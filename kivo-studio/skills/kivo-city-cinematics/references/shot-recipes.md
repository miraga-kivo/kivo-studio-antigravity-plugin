# Shot design and review

## Character introduction (compact proof)

An approximately twelve-second edit: a readable map for 2.5 seconds, a city image for 3 seconds, a five-second character follow clip, a 1.5-second endcard. Only five seconds are model-generated animation. This keeps a useful reference→Draft→final validation affordable; use a moving city shot when a fuller trailer is wanted.

Fix geography, time and outfit first. Create an environment plate, then create the character inside that environment. Use both images in the video prompt with their distinct roles. A rear or three-quarter view works well for a third-person exploration shot without relying on facial resemblance. Use adult original characters unless the user asks otherwise. One plausible action, one camera move and no cuts are easier to compare than multiple unrelated actions in five seconds.

Do not promise that the image model exactly reconstructs the schematic map. Check whether coastline, river, terrain and major relative districts broadly agree. The mini-map is a conceptual city graphic, not a tracking system: it cannot automatically infer the character's exact world-space position. Keep the marker fixed unless motion has actually been authored.

## City trailer

A fifteen-second edit: map 2 seconds, generated aerial city clip 4 seconds, character clip 5 seconds, endcard 4 seconds. The city clip uses the environment reference; the hero clip uses environment + character. Quote both batches and both selected finals before committing to a budget. A budget approved for the compact proof does not automatically cover this mode. If the budget is short, offer the static city plate or fewer candidates.

For longer films, extend the shot list deliberately; do not stretch a five-second animation to a claimed minute of unique content. Keep raw final clips separate so the user can edit them later.

## Practical quality gate

Review actual downloaded clips, with time-spaced frames and playback when available:

- Is the scene a coherent rendered city rather than an unrelated fantasy painting? Does the first location match the environment palette and geography?
- Is the original character's jacket, bag, hair, body proportion and footwear recognisable throughout? Inspect limbs while walking.
- Does a slow camera remain steady? Any sudden cut, teleport, texture warp or scenery replacement?
- Does finalisation preserve the selected candidate? Compare matching timestamps, not unrelated frames.
- Are graphic names correct, escape-safe and legible on phone-sized previews? Is the HUD clear of the character and the shoreline? Remove the overlay if it distracts.

Select on these criteria rather than on resolution alone. Rework the brief or references when identity or geography is wrong; rerunning the same prompt without a diagnosis is not a reliable fix. Record a result as partial if visuals or permissions prevented live validation. Never label a synthetic fixture or unrun plan as a model-generated demonstration.
